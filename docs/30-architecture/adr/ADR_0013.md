# ADR_0013 — Ring 3 SIGCANCEL Cancellation Points and Recursive stdio Locks

```{adr} Ring 3 SIGCANCEL cancellation points and recursive stdio locks
:id: ADR_0013
:status: accepted

## Context

`SWR_LIBC_0004` requires POSIX.1-2024 XSH 2.9.5 deferred cancellation across
blocking `libc` calls (`pthread_cond_wait`, `read`, `write`, `close`, `ppoll`,
`sem_wait`, `clock_nanosleep`, `waitpid`, `aio_suspend`, and `pthread_join`)
with cleanup handlers and no held internal locks. Concurrent threads also need
recursive `<stdio.h>` stream locking (`flockfile`, `ftrylockfile`,
`funlockfile`).

## Decision

- Keep cancellation state (`cancel_disabled`, `cancel_async`, `cancel_pending`,
  `cleanup_head`) in per-thread `FS.base` TLS (`__pthread_tls_t`) with zero new
  kernel syscalls.
- Reserve internal signal `30` (`LIBC_SIGCANCEL`) with an unmasked `SA_NODEFER`
  handler installed at `__libc_init_main_tls()`. `pthread_cancel()` sets
  `cancel_pending` and injects `LIBC_SIGCANCEL` to wake blocked threads with
  `-EINTR`.
- Check `__libc_cancel_point()` on entry and `-EINTR` exit in all mandatory
  cancellation points. Re-acquire the caller's mutex in `pthread_cond_clockwait`
  and decrement `sem->__waiters` in `sem_clockwait` before acting on
  cancellation.
- Back `stdin`, `stdout`, and `stderr` with recursive `pthread_mutex_t` locks
  and wrap locked stdio functions in `pthread_cleanup_push`/`pop` so canceled
  threads always unlock the stream.

## Rejected alternatives

- Adding dedicated Ring 0 cancellation syscalls and kernel cleanup stacks.
- Polling `cancel_pending` with short timeouts instead of waking blocked
  syscalls through `SYS_THREAD_INJECT_SIG`.

## Consequences

- `canceltest` verifies `pthread_cond_wait` mutex re-acquisition, LIFO cleanup
  handlers, `PTHREAD_CANCEL_DISABLE`, blocking cancellation points, and
  recursive `flockfile`/`funlockfile` serialization in QEMU.
- All async-signal-safe wrappers remain free of forbidden heap or stdio callees.
```
