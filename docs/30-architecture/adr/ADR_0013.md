# ADR_0013 — Ring 3 SIGCANCEL Cancellation Points and Recursive stdio Mutexes

```{adr} Ring 3 SIGCANCEL cancellation points and recursive stdio mutexes
:id: ADR_0013
:status: accepted

## Context

For `SWR_LIBC_0004`, the system must give POSIX.1-2024 XSH 2.9.5 deferred
cancellation across synchronous `libc` calls (`pthread_cond_wait`, `read`,
`write`, `close`, `ppoll`, `sem_wait`, `clock_nanosleep`, `waitpid`,
`aio_suspend`, and `pthread_join`) with cleanup handlers and no locked internal
mutexes. Concurrent threads must also have recursive `<stdio.h>` stream mutexes
(`flockfile`, `ftrylockfile`, `funlockfile`).

## Decision

- Thread `FS.base` TLS (`__pthread_tls_t`) keeps cancellation state
  (`cancel_disabled`, `cancel_async`, `cancel_pending`, `cleanup_head`) with no
  new kernel syscalls.
- `__libc_init_main_tls()` installs an unmasked `SA_NODEFER` handler on internal
  signal `30` (`LIBC_SIGCANCEL`). `pthread_cancel()` sets `cancel_pending` and
  sends `LIBC_SIGCANCEL` to wake blocked threads with `-EINTR`.
- All cancellation points call `__libc_cancel_point()` on entry and on `-EINTR`
  exit. `pthread_cond_clockwait` locks the caller mutex again and
  `sem_clockwait` decreases `sem->__waiters` before cancellation stops the
  thread.
- `stdin`, `stdout`, and `stderr` use recursive `pthread_mutex_t` mutexes.
  Locked stdio functions use `pthread_cleanup_push` and `pthread_cleanup_pop`.
  Thus canceled threads always unlock the stream.

## Rejected alternatives

- Kernel cancellation syscalls and Ring 0 cleanup stacks.
- Short-timeout loops on `cancel_pending` as an alternative to
  `SYS_THREAD_INJECT_SIG`.

## Results

- `canceltest` does tests of `pthread_cond_wait` mutex lock recovery, LIFO
  cleanup handlers, `PTHREAD_CANCEL_DISABLE`, synchronous cancellation points,
  and recursive `flockfile` and `funlockfile` serialization in QEMU.
- All async-signal-safe wrappers stay free of heap or stdio calls.
```
